Q2 — Feature to REMOVE / deprecate (paste)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
On NEJA (the events app I own), I want to remove — and have already started killing — any checkout path that invents a “Payment Complete” / wallet ticket after we only open the organiser’s affiliate ticket link.

Why: it creates false confidence. Guests think NEJA charged them or that a door-ready pass exists when neither is true. That’s worse than a missing feature; it’s a trust bug dressed as success UX.

Why it existed / why it lingered: early launch pressure favored a complete-feeling funnel (Buy → success → Wallet) over honesty about our affiliate model. Deprecating it means rewriting copy, stopping auto wallet creation, and accepting a less “pretty” success state (“Opened the organiser’s site — NEJA didn’t charge you”) until real payments exist. I delayed because shipping felt like progress; user trust and commercial clarity matter more. Cutting misleading success states is product work, not polish.

Q3 — Decision in last 6 months I’d make differently (paste)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
I’d reverse the decision to keep an in-app “Payment Method” / card UI path alive while NEJA still doesn’t take card payments.

At the time it felt like future-proofing and looking like a real checkout. What changed my mind was launch-audit reality: dead ends when an event has no ticket URL, mock Visa rows on free tickets, and copy that implied secure NEJA checkout. That burns trust faster than a simpler “open organiser link / free ticket to Wallet” flow.

I’d decide earlier: one honest paid path (organiser URL or hide Buy) and one honest free path — no decorative payment chrome. Supporting a senior PM on a client, I’d push the same way: don’t ship theatre that contradicts the commercial model.
