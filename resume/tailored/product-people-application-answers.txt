Q2 — Feature to REMOVE / deprecate (paste)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
On NEJA, the events app I own, I’d remove the checkout path that can still say Payment Complete and invent a wallet ticket after we only open an organiser’s affiliate link.

I didn’t ship that as a clever trick. As a first-time founder building something Kosovo doesn’t have yet, I kept moving between two futures: take payments in the app, or start with organiser ticket links and earn the right to charge later. While I was still deciding, the product wore a “purchase finished” shape. Unfinished commerce wanted a finished feeling.

I’m removing it because people believe what the screen tells them. If it says paid, they think they paid. That taught me the product habit I want to bring into a product role: protect trust before you protect how polished the funnel looks. Commit to how the product actually works, then design the screen. Don’t decorate a decision you haven’t made.

Why it stayed: I wouldn’t cut the misleading state until the honest replacement existed, clear handoff copy and a path that doesn’t fake a ticket. I’ve made the affiliate-first call now. The cut is in progress. The feature lasts only as long as that replacement takes, and not one day longer.

Q3 — Decision in last 6 months I’d make differently (paste)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
I’d change how I entered product. For too long I treated building my own app as the only proof I belonged, and I delayed calling myself product until everything looked finished. Coming from analysis, ops, and recruiting, that felt safer than saying I was still learning in public.

What changed my mind was watching myself polish unfinished decisions instead of owning them. I kept trying to look complete while I was still choosing the real model. That is a career habit, not just a shipping habit: wait, decorate, then arrive.

I’d do it differently. Enter earlier. Be honest about what I know and what I’m still deciding. Learn with teams, feedback, and messy client work, not only by founding alone. The product skill I want to be hired for is judgment under uncertainty, not a perfect founder story.
